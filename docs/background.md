# Background

HIV-1 protease is a dynamic drug target in which resistance-associated mutations can alter inhibitor response without necessarily producing large changes in the average protein structure. Because protease function depends on transitions between conformational states that regulate access to the active site, a mutation may instead modify the accessibility and population of alternative states.

This project was motivated by the question of how a resistance-associated sequence change can reshape the dynamic ensemble of a protein and, consequently, its functionally relevant conformational states. Rather than treating structural differences as changes between single representative conformations, the analysis focuses on the ensemble: identifying collective motions, resolving populated regions of conformational space, and connecting these regions to physically interpretable descriptors of protease dynamics.

The completed WT–I50V analysis therefore asks a broader mechanistic question:

> Does resistance-associated variation primarily reorganize the structure of the protein, or does it redistribute the populations and accessibility of functionally relevant conformational states?
